### Approaches

1. **Single monolithic completion pass** — expand all contracts, providers, UI routes, management, marketplace, Iberá-Alerta, worker scheduling, and real verification in one implementation stream.
   - Pros: one apparent product milestone and fewer temporary seams.
   - Cons: crosses unrelated data ownership and risk boundaries, makes migrations and provider failures hard to isolate, encourages false readiness claims, and creates an oversized review and rollback unit.
   - Effort: Very High

2. **Evidence-first vertical slices with a shared completion gate** — establish canonical location/evidence/auth boundaries, then independently deliver field selection, climate/SMN/satellite ingestion, normalized frontend evidence, authorized Copilot, Iberá-Alerta recovery/readiness, management, safe marketplace discovery/RFQ, and finally a real-service cross-product matrix.
   - Pros: preserves current architecture, allows each slice to be compiled/tested/smoked independently, isolates provider and migration risk, keeps Iberá-Alerta separate, and makes readiness claims evidence-based.
   - Cons: requires explicit contract versioning and sequencing discipline; the full product milestone arrives after several slices.
   - Effort: High, but tractable

3. **Frontend-first route/view completion** — add missing pages and visual states before wiring providers, durable contracts, and business operations.
   - Pros: fast visible progress and useful route inventory.
   - Cons: would reproduce mock/demo behavior, hide missing backend permissions and persistence, and fail the requested real-service verification goal.
   - Effort: Medium initially, High rework risk

### Recommendation

Proceed with Approach 2. Treat this as a mega-SDD roadmap whose implementation is split into independently verifiable slices, not as one unchecked mega-patch. The first proposal/spec should freeze the product boundary and canonical evidence model, then sequence the following prerequisites and slices:

1. **Baseline and boundary gate (prerequisite):** inventory current routes/contracts/provider modes; preserve the immutable auth change; define product ownership between Agronautas and Iberá-Alerta; define source-backed freshness/readiness vocabulary; prohibit demo fallback in real mode; record rollback and evidence requirements.
2. **Location and field-selection slice (prerequisite):** make authorized workspace/field selection truthful; support point/polygon selection through a real map/provider only when configured, with a non-cartographic fallback; validate field/workspace scope and location lineage; expose one stable selected-location contract to all downstream queries.
3. **Climate, weather-alert, SMN, and satellite evidence slice (prerequisite for Copilot):** wire real provider adapters/processors, source-specific time semantics, normalized signal/evidence envelopes, per-location persistence, idempotent scheduled windows, freshness/degradation/fallback rules, and source/run lineage. Satellite must not be represented as live until scene/coverage/processing evidence exists.
4. **Evidence dashboard/frontend slice:** render weather, SMN alerts, climate, satellite indices, source URL, observed/acquired/retrieved times, freshness, provider mode, confidence, lineage, and explicit unavailable/degraded states from one backend contract; remove misleading mock/demo signals from protected real flows.
5. **Authorized evidence-only Copilot slice:** construct Copilot context from the authorized workspace/field and current approved source records only; require freshness and source lineage; cite evidence/run IDs; reject unsupported or missing evidence; ensure field Copilot cannot receive unrelated municipal, marketplace, auth, or stale cross-workspace context. Keep Iberá official-source Copilot separate and read-only.
6. **Iberá-Alerta completion slice:** finish per-location state, timeline, source freshness, ingest recovery/retry/lease behavior, terminal failure/partial semantics, operator permissions, geometry/coverage truth, and readiness presentation. Preserve its PNA/INA/INMET/SMN boundary and explicit exclusions; reuse only versioned read-only evidence where approved.
7. **Management-system slice:** define durable campaigns/seasons, field operations/tasks, responsibility/permission checks, activity/audit semantics, and actual business paths before adding views. Keep planning state explicit; do not silently turn assumption simulation into verified economics.
8. **Safe marketplace slice:** begin with local B2B catalog/listings/discovery and human-reviewed RFQ/offer handoff states if approved; define participant/workspace ownership, availability/quality/traceability and permissions. No custody, settlement, financial guarantees, or regulated/export claims in this change.
9. **Real-service verification gate:** run compiled API/Web/worker paths against authorized real services and endpoints, source by source and product by product; verify database persistence, queue/cron transitions, BFF/auth boundaries, browser rendering, recovery/degraded states, and production separately from local. Unit mocks and route stubs remain supplemental only.

Real-service prerequisites should be documented by category only: PostgreSQL/PostGIS and migrations, Redis/queue, worker DSN and contract root, BFF/API origins and CORS, Agronautas auth/session/bootstrap configuration, map/geocoding provider configuration, Open-Meteo commercial-use approval where required, SMN endpoint/policy, NASA FIRMS key, Sentinel/Copernicus credentials or approved STAC provider, Groq model/key for Copilot, hydrology ingest token/cron owner, and Render service/deployment/log access. No secret values belong in the artifact.

Explicit non-goals for the proposal/spec: activating payments, payouts, Checkout Pro, Money Out, escrow, settlement, or marketplace financial custody; copying Alqui or Vialovers product behavior into Agronautas without a new approved scope; reopening or modifying `agronautas-auth-security-isolation`; changing its receipts/freezes/hashes or attempting verify/archive; replacing Iberá-Alerta’s institutional product boundary; hydraulic routing, dam discharge, propagation-time, or evacuation-authority claims; and claiming production readiness without real-service evidence.

### Risks

- **Scope and review risk:** “all product flows” spans provider ingestion, contracts, worker/queue, auth/workspace, two product surfaces, management, and a new marketplace domain. Tasks must remain independently reviewable and forecast the configured single-PR size exception before implementation.
- **Contract drift:** current Agronautas weather, satellite, dashboard, Copilot, hydrology, and Python contracts use different semantics and versions. A canonical evidence/lineage migration is required before frontend and Copilot completion.
- **False freshness or provenance:** adapters and fallback jobs can produce typed data without proving provider success, scene coverage, source status, or currentness. Provider mode, schema/HTTP evidence, last-success timestamps, and raw lineage must remain visible.
- **Scheduler/worker gap:** the scheduled-window worker intentionally returns unavailable and Render does not currently declare an Agronautas source cron. End-to-end ingestion cannot be claimed until scheduling, leases, retries, durable outcomes, and real provider processors are proven.
- **Geospatial correctness:** current field coverage is Corrientes/AR-W-oriented and Iberá municipality geometry is unverified. A map UI alone must not imply territorial correctness or general coverage.
- **Authorization leakage:** Copilot, workspace fields, planning, and future marketplace operations must enforce actor/workspace/field scope in backend use cases and repositories, not only in frontend queries.
- **Product-boundary contamination:** Agronautas agricultural evidence, Iberá-Alerta institutional hydrology, and marketplace business workflows have different owners, claims, and permissions. Shared adapters must be versioned and read-only across boundaries.
- **External-service variability:** SMN access, Sentinel licensing/availability, FIRMS credentials, Groq limits, map providers, Render cron, and production database/Redis state can fail independently. Each slice needs source-specific degradation and recovery evidence.
- **Dirty-checkout authority risk:** the current checkout contains unrelated/in-progress auth and marketplace-package artifacts. Implementation phases must preserve them and never infer clean-baseline or production conclusions from uncommitted files without explicit scope.
